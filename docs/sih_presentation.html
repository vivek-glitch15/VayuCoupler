Scientific Rigor & Formulations

How Weather–Pollution Coupling Works: The Mathematics

Verifiable, transparent atmospheric equations replacing opaque black-box machine learning.

1. VENTILATION INDEX (VI) — FLUSHING CAPACITY
VI = Wind_Speed (m/s) × PBL_Height (m) [m²/s]
VI > 3,500 m²/s: Favorable dispersion; pollutants flush out rapidly.
VI < 2,000 m²/s: Critical Trapping; Delhi basin behaves as an enclosed container.
Nighttime boundary layer collapse causes 3× to 5× pollutant density accumulation.
2. THERMAL INVERSION TRAPPING COEFFICIENT (K_trap)
K_trap = 1.0 + 0.38(ΔT_inv) + 1.4 × max(0, (2500 - VI)/2500)
Quantifies the thermal 'lid' formed over Delhi NCR during cold winter nights.
ΔT_inv: Temperature differential between upper boundary (1000m) and surface.
When K_trap > 2.2, standard emissions produce 'Severe+' emergency spikes.
3. UPWIND STUBBLE TRANSPORT VECTOR (S_vector)
S_vector = FireCount × max(0, cos(θ_wind - 315°)) × (WS / 5.0)
Projects the directional dot-product of wind with the NW stubble corridor (315°).
Only fires with aligned north-westerly wind vectors are transported into Delhi.
Directly computes particulate mass influx (µg/m³) rather than static correlations.
4. COUPLED FORECASTER & SOURCE ATTRIBUTION
AQI(t+Δt) = F_phys(AQI_t, VI, K_trap, S_vector) ± 90% CI
Outputs continuous +24h, +48h, and +72h predictive envelopes with 90% confidence bands.
Dynamic source apportionment: Stubble (38%), Vehicular (32%), Industry (15%), Dust (15%).
Directly feeds the automated CAQM rules.json trigger matrix.
